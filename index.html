About the Workshop

Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Artificial Intelligence (AI) hold immense potential to transform healthcare communication, particularly in linguistically diverse and resource-constrained environments. Jointly organized by CMC Vellore and IIIT Hyderabad (LTRC), the NLP-AI4Health workshop provides an interdisciplinary platform to advance inclusive, accessible, and culturally aware language technologies for healthcare.

Building upon the success of previous editions, this edition places a special focus on code-mixed Indian language doctor-patient interactions. Real-world clinical encounters in multilingual regions like India rarely occur in a single monolingual format; instead, patients and healthcare providers frequently mix local languages (e.g., Hindi, Bengali, Kannada, Marathi, Tamil, Telugu) with English medical terminology and local colloquialisms. Processing such non-standard, code-mixed conversational data presents unique computational challenges for information extraction, clinical documentation, and automated dialogue understanding.

We invite research contributions on low-resource medical NLP, simplification of clinical documents, speech-based tools for Electronic Health Records (EHRs), processing code-mixed clinical dialogues, and ethically aligned, culturally sensitive health AI systems. The workshop brings together NLP researchers, computer scientists, clinicians, healthcare practitioners, and policy experts to co-create practical and impactful language solutions for diverse healthcare settings.

Workshop Objectives

The primary goal of NLP-AI4Health is to bridge the gap between computational linguistics and clinical healthcare needs in multilingual and resource-constrained environments.

  • Understand: Analyze real-world communication challenges and linguistic complexities in code-mixed and low-resource doctor-patient interactions.
  • Build: Develop computational tools and models to simplify, transcribe, and translate patient-facing documents (e.g., consent forms, discharge summaries, and prescriptions).
  • Improve: Advance speech and text-based AI models for automated clinical documentation, SOAP note generation, and multilingual Electronic Health Record (EHR) entry.
  • Promote: Foster collaborative interdisciplinary partnerships between healthcare professionals, clinicians, NLP researchers, and policymakers.
  • Organize: Host benchmark shared tasks on code-mixed Indian language doctor-patient conversations to drive state-of-the-art research in low-resource health NLP.

Call for Papers

We invite original research papers, short papers, system descriptions, and non-archival position papers on topics at the intersection of NLP, AI, and healthcare communication — with particular emphasis on Indian languages and low-resource multilingual settings.

Research Topics of Interest

  • Code-Mixed & Conversational Health NLP: Processing, parsing, and normalizing code-mixed doctor-patient dialogues in Indian languages.
  • Clinical Information Extraction & Term Identification: Named Entity Recognition (NER) and Medical Term Identification (MTI) from spoken and transcribed consultations.
  • Automated Clinical Documentation: Dialogue-to-SOAP note generation, clinical record synthesis, and automated discharge summary generation.
  • Medical Dialogue Summarization: Abstractive and extractive summarization of doctor-patient interactions.
  • Low-Resource & Multilingual Medical Machine Translation: Translation of medical texts, patient queries, and clinical records across Indian languages.
  • Medical Question Answering & Dialogue Systems: Conversational AI, virtual health assistants, and QA systems tailored to patient care.
  • Health Misinformation & Fact-Checking: Detection and mitigation of health-related fake news and misinformation in regional languages.
  • Speech-Based Health Tools: ASR, TTS, and voice-enabled EHR input tools for low-literacy and clinical contexts.
  • Ethics, Privacy & Cultural Sensitivity in Health AI: De-identification of patient data, bias mitigation, fairness, and safety in healthcare AI.

Shared Tasks

Shared Task on Code-Mixed Indian Language Doctor-Patient Dialogue Processing
(CM-DocHealth 2027)

Real-world doctor-patient consultations in India are inherently code-mixed, capturing spontaneous exchanges between clinicians and patients in regional languages combined with English medical jargon. This edition's shared task introduces a benchmark dataset of Code-Mixed Indian Language Doctor-Patient Dialogues across six major Indian languages: Hindi, Bengali, Kannada, Marathi, Tamil, and Telugu.

Participants will be invited to tackle three sub-tasks:

Sub-Task 1: Medical Term Identification (MTI) in Code-Mixed Conversations

Given code-mixed doctor-patient dialogue transcripts, participants must accurately identify, extract, and categorize medical terms (e.g., symptoms, diagnoses, medications, anatomical sites, and procedures).

Key Challenge: Handling non-standard spelling, colloquial disease descriptors, code-switching between regional languages and English, and phonetic transliterations.

Sub-Task 2: Automated SOAP Note Generation from Clinical Dialogues

Given a doctor-patient interaction, participants must automatically structure the conversation into a standardized clinical SOAP Note:

  • S (Subjective): Patient's reported chief complaints and history.
  • O (Objective): Vital signs, physical examination findings, and test results.
  • A (Assessment): Preliminary or differential diagnosis.
  • P (Plan): Prescribed treatment, medication, diagnostic tests, and follow-up advice.

Key Challenge: Synthesizing unstructured, code-mixed dialogue into formal, structured medical summaries.

Sub-Task 3: Medical Dialogue Summarization

Participants must generate concise, coherent, and clinically accurate summaries of code-mixed doctor-patient consultations suitable for patient discharge sheets or physician quick-reviews.

Key Challenge: Ensuring factual accuracy (hallucination prevention), retaining critical clinical details, and summarizing code-mixed input into standardized target outputs.

Detailed task guidelines, evaluation metrics, baseline systems, and dataset release timelines will be released soon.

Submission Guidelines

Submissions must follow the ACL formatting guidelines (LaTeX style files or Word template). All submissions are double-blind reviewed and must be anonymized.

  • Long Papers: up to 8 pages of content + unlimited references
  • Short Papers: up to 4 pages of content + unlimited references
  • Shared Task Papers: up to 4 pages + unlimited references
  • Extended Abstracts (non-archival): up to 2 pages

The submission portal link (OpenReview / ARR) will be activated once the workshop is assigned to a conference. ARR-reviewed papers may be committed to this workshop. At least one author must register and present the accepted paper.

All submissions must be in English. Research may cover any Indian language(s) including code-mixed and transliterated data.
